The whole strategy in one sentence

Don't make Cracker Barrel cool. Make what's already cool about Cracker Barrel impossible to ignore.

The peg game. The rockers. The biscuits. The weird thing hanging on the wall that somebody absolutely wants the backstory on. The road-trip stop your family has made for 20 years. Social doesn't need to manufacture a personality for this brand. The personality is already everywhere.

Today's Menu

Cracker Barrel guests don't just follow the brand. They feel like it's theirs. The last year made that impossible to miss. Guests spoke loudly about what they associate with Cracker Barrel, the company listened, and the conversation around the brand became much bigger than a logo.

And, genuinely: what social media person wouldn't want to tackle that?

I don't mean chasing controversy or trying to relitigate it. I mean the opportunity underneath it. This is a nationally recognized brand with decades of memory attached to it, a community willing to tell you exactly what it loves, and a social role specifically responsible for sentiment, voice, reactive opportunities and early PR signals.

That's fascinating work. The goal isn't to be afraid of a passionate comment section. It's to understand it: separate noise from signal, make real guests feel heard, protect what people already love, and find culturally relevant ways to invite new people onto the porch without pretending the porch needs to become something else.

Content that travels

At San Antonio Zoo I ran social for a 6M+ audience: reactive Reels and TikToks, seasonal events, influencer partnerships, launches and a calendar kept weeks ahead without losing the ability to move fast.

PR instincts built in

I owned PR and social together. I wasn't just asking whether a post would perform; I was watching sentiment, media implications and deciding what needed a reply, a DM, or leadership attention.

The opportunity I see: don't run from how much people care about Cracker Barrel. Build the social strategy around it. Listen better than anyone, respond like a human, and give people new reasons to care without sanding away the things that made them care in the first place.
